Best Air Fryers for Everyday Cooking in 2026

We cooked fries, chicken thighs, salmon, and roasted vegetables in five of the most-bought air fryers on Amazon — from a $69 mini to an 8-quart dual-basket family unit. After four weeks, one model clearly earned the top spot on our counter.

Updated: September 21, 2026 · 5 air fryers tested · 28 days of testing

The air fryer is the small kitchen appliance that genuinely changed how we cook at home. It preheats in three minutes, uses a fraction of the oil of a deep fryer, and leaves behind a basket that's quicker to clean than a sheet pan. For couples and small families, it has quietly replaced the oven for most weeknight dinners.

The catch: "air fryer" now covers everything from $30 basket models to $400 dual-zone smart ovens. Some are brilliant. Some take 22 minutes to crisp a single chicken breast. We ordered five of the most-purchased models on Amazon — across budget, mid-range, and premium tiers — and used each one as our default for at least three nights per week.

How we tested

Every air fryer was unboxed, washed, and run through the same three-night test menu in two home kitchens (London and Toronto):

  • Fries — frozen shoestring fries from a single bag, cooked from frozen at the recommended program. We measured crispness by visual inspection and how easily a fork pierced the center.
  • Chicken thighs — bone-in, skin-on, room temperature. We judged even browning, internal doneness, and how much smoke the unit produced.
  • Salmon fillet — skin-on, 6 oz. We looked for a crispy skin and a tender interior (no chalky mid-section).

Beyond the test menu, we tracked: preheat time, noise level at one meter (a free phone decibel meter app is fine for this), basket non-stick durability after a month of use, exterior heat, and how easy the basket and tray were to wash by hand and in a dishwasher.

Crispiest results: Ninja AF181 Max XL 6.5 Qt

If crunch is your religion, the AF181 is the machine. Its MaxCrisp mode runs at 450°F from start to finish — most air fryers only touch that temperature during preheat — and in our chicken-thigh test it produced shatter-crisp skin that no other model here matched, the COSORI included. It also carries around 90,000 reviews averaging 4.7 stars, which makes it one of the most-validated small appliances on Amazon.

The trade-offs are size and price: the square 6.5-quart body eats counter space and typically runs $30–40 more than the TurboBlaze. If you mostly reheat and roast rather than chase maximum crunch, save the money and buy the COSORI.

Family pick: Ninja DZ201 DualZone 8 Qt

Two independent baskets mean you can cook fries at 400°F in one and salmon at 380°F in the other — and the Smart Finish feature makes both finish at the same moment. For families, this eliminates the single biggest complaint about air fryers: that they cook too little at once. Build quality is excellent and both baskets go in the dishwasher without warping.

It's the most expensive pick on this page ($160–200), so we only recommend it for households of 3+ who will genuinely use both zones. For everyone else, one 6-quart basket plus a little patience costs half as much.

The ClearCook adds a viewing window and interior light to the standard 6-quart basket formula. It sounds like a gimmick; in practice it's the feature we missed most when switching back to windowless units — watching fries turn golden instead of pulling the basket six times is genuinely useful. Instant's OdorErase filter also knocked down cooking smells noticeably better than any other model here.

Cooking performance sits a small step behind the Ninja and COSORI — a minute or two slower to the same crispness — but with 4.6 stars across tens of thousands of reviews, it's far from a compromise. Pick it if you cook smelly things (fish, wings) in a small apartment.

Best budget: Instant Vortex Mini 4 Qt

At $69 — and frequently less on sale — the Vortex Mini is the best entry point into air frying. The 4-quart basket handles two servings of fries or four chicken tenders, it takes less counter space than a toaster, and it uses the same simple dial controls as its bigger sibling. For one-person households, dorms, and offices, it's the obvious buy.

What we didn't pick — and why

The Breville Smart Oven Air Fryer Pro is a superb toaster-oven-plus-air- fryer combo, but at $400+ it answers a different question — "should I replace my second oven?" — than the one most buyers are asking.

We also tested three sub-$35 no-name models. None of them lasted the month without the non-stick coating flaking or the fan rattling. We don't recommend any air fryer under $35 for daily use.

FAQ

Are air fryers healthier than ovens?

For most foods, air fryers use 70–80% less oil than deep frying. Versus a conventional oven, they don't necessarily make food "healthier" — but they preheat in 3 minutes instead of 15, which means less time running the kitchen hot. For weeknight cooking, that's the real win.

How big should my air fryer be?

A single basket of 5–6 quarts is right for most 2-person households. For families of 3+, look at dual-basket models (8+ total quarts) or accept that you'll cook in batches.

Do air fryers smoke?

Quality air fryers don't, even with skin-on chicken. If yours is smoking, the most likely culprits are residual oil from previous cooks in the heating element, or overcrowding the basket so drippings hit the element directly. Clean the element every 8–10 cooks and you'll be fine.

Are dishwasher-safe baskets actually safe?

Yes — for all the picks above. We ran 30+ dishwasher cycles on each basket and saw no warping or coating loss. Avoid steel wool or abrasive sponges; the non-stick surfaces don't need them.
